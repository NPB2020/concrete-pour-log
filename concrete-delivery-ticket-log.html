Concrete Delivery Ticket Log

A delivery log is most useful when the ticket, timing, quantity, observations and supporting photo for each truck stay connected.

Field-documentation guide. This page is a practical recordkeeping resource, not a project specification, engineering instruction, compliance certification or substitute for your approved QA/QC procedures.
01

Use one record per delivery

Give every truck a distinct delivery entry. The ticket or delivery number is the strongest reference for connecting the physical ticket, field notes, quantities, temperatures and later questions about that load.

02

Core delivery fields

Useful fields include ticket number, truck number where applicable, delivered volume, batch or arrival time, discharge start and finish, concrete temperature, delivery status and a short note. Your project may require additional information.

03

Photograph the ticket clearly

Capture the entire ticket at a readable angle and associate the image with the correct delivery. Avoid relying on a camera roll full of unlabelled ticket photos that must be reconstructed after the pour.

04

Track quantity as the pour progresses

Maintain ordered and delivered quantities and truck count as deliveries are accepted or adjusted. This gives the site team a current view of progress and provides a useful cross-check at closeout.

05

Record exceptions with the delivery

If a truck is delayed, adjusted, rejected or otherwise involved in an issue, make the relationship clear in the field record. Record what happened and the action taken rather than relying on a later recollection.

06

Keep the delivery log in the final report

A useful final pour report lets a reviewer follow individual deliveries and see their associated ticket references, timing, quantities, observations and supporting evidence in context.
