Concrete Delivery Ticket Log

The simplest way to make a concrete delivery traceable is to keep its ticket reference, quantity, timing, observations and source image together.

Field-documentation resource. Use the contract documents, approved mix requirements, inspection/test plan and applicable standards for your project. This guide organizes records; it does not establish acceptance criteria or certify concrete quality.

What is a concrete delivery ticket log?

It is a chronological record of the concrete deliveries associated with a pour. Rather than treating the supplier ticket as a loose piece of paper, the log gives each delivery a searchable reference and connects the information you need on site with the original ticket image.

A delivery log is one part of the wider concrete pour documentation workflow.

Five mistakes that make delivery logs harder to use

Ticket photos only in the camera roll

A photo without a delivery reference creates matching work later.

One total instead of truck-level records

A final volume alone cannot explain which delivery an issue or test relates to.

Inconsistent units

Keep the project in a consistent unit system and label values clearly.

Vague issue notes

Identify the delivery and write what happened and what action was taken.

Rebuilding the log after the pour

Capture records as deliveries occur, when the source information is in front of you.

From ticket log to final pour report

The delivery log should not be an isolated spreadsheet that must be copied into another document. It is most useful when it feeds the final pour report together with tests, issues and photos. Before closeout, use the QA/QC documentation checklist to identify gaps.

Delivery log FAQ

Do I need a truck number if I have a ticket number?

The ticket/delivery number is normally the stronger unique reference. A truck number can still be useful as a secondary identifier where your supplier uses it.

Should I photograph every delivery ticket?

Follow your project procedure. Where ticket images are part of the record, attach them to the matching delivery rather than storing them without context.

Does a delivery log prove the concrete was accepted?

No. It records the delivery. Acceptance depends on the project requirements and responsible personnel.
