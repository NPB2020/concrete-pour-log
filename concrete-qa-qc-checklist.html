Concrete QA/QC Checklist

Use this checklist to organize the records around a concrete pour. It complements — but does not replace — the project specification, inspection/test plan or qualified QA/QC personnel.

Field-documentation resource. Use the contract documents, approved mix requirements, inspection/test plan and applicable standards for your project. This guide organizes records; it does not establish acceptance criteria or certify concrete quality.

Before the pour

  • Confirm project, pour element/location, planned date and responsible field contacts.
  • Confirm supplier, mix reference and ordered quantity against the approved project information.
  • Review the project specification and inspection/test plan for required inspections, tests, specimens and records.
  • Confirm the placement area and required pre-pour inspections have been released by the responsible project personnel.
  • Confirm the field-testing plan: who performs the tests, where results are recorded and how samples/specimens are identified.
  • Set up a consistent method for delivery tickets and site photos before trucks begin arriving.

During deliveries and placement

  • Create a separate record for each delivery/ticket.
  • Record delivered volume and relevant truck/ticket references.
  • Capture required arrival, discharge or other project-specific times.
  • Keep ticket images associated with the correct delivery.
  • Record fresh-concrete observations/tests required by the project.
  • Track running delivered quantity and truck count.
  • Document interruptions, rejected/adjusted deliveries or other notable events when they occur.

For the truck-level structure, see the Concrete Delivery Ticket Log.

Field testing records

Testing requirements vary by project. ACI’s Field Testing Technician—Grade I program specifically covers properly performing and recording basic fresh-concrete field tests, while ASTM maintains current standards covering sampling, temperature, slump, density/yield and air content. Your project documents determine what applies and what constitutes acceptance. ACI reference ↗ · ASTM standards catalog ↗

  • Identify the test type and the delivery/pour it relates to.
  • Record result, unit, time and sample/specimen reference where applicable.
  • Record the person/agency information required by the project procedure.
  • Keep field notes factual; do not infer acceptance from an isolated result unless authorized by the applicable procedure.

Issues, deviations and supporting photos

  • Describe what happened in factual language.
  • Identify the relevant delivery, location or stage of placement.
  • Record the action taken and status where applicable.
  • Add photos that clarify the event; use captions instead of relying on memory.
  • Escalate and obtain approvals through the project’s established QA/QC process.

Before closeout and handover

  • Reconcile truck count and recorded delivered quantity.
  • Check expected ticket references/images for gaps.
  • Check expected temperatures, times and field-test entries.
  • Confirm issues and actions are understandable to someone who was not on site.
  • Check general pour photos and captions.
  • Leave unavailable information visibly “not recorded” rather than guessing.
  • Generate or assemble the final field record and route it for the required project review.

Why the testing disclaimer matters: ACI states that applicable concrete tests should be conducted by individuals certified in the relevant categories, and many specifications/agencies require ACI certification. A recordkeeping app is therefore a documentation tool — not a replacement for testing qualifications or formal QA review. ACI technical FAQ ↗

QA/QC checklist FAQ

Is this a concrete inspection and test plan (ITP)?

No. It is a field-documentation checklist. Your project’s approved ITP/specification governs inspection points, test frequency, acceptance criteria and responsibilities.

Does the checklist determine whether a load passes or fails?

No. It helps you check whether the relevant record exists. Acceptance is determined under the applicable project requirements.
