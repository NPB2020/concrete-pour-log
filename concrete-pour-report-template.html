FIELD DOCUMENTATION GUIDE

Concrete Pour Report Template

A useful pour report should make it easy to reconstruct what happened on site — without searching through notebooks, ticket photos and messages.

Field-documentation guide. This page is a practical recordkeeping resource, not a project specification, engineering instruction, compliance certification or substitute for your approved QA/QC procedures.
01

What a concrete pour report should capture

At minimum, keep the project and pour identity, date and location, concrete supplier and mix reference, ordered and delivered volume, delivery records, testing information, issues or deviations, notes and supporting photos together. The goal is a traceable field record — not a substitute for your project specification, laboratory testing or formal QA approval.

02

1. Project and pour details

Record the project, pour location or element, date, supplier, mix reference and ordered quantity. Use a clear pour name so the record can be found later without relying on memory.

03

2. Delivery log

For each truck, record the delivery or ticket number, truck number where useful, delivered volume, batch/arrival/discharge times when required, concrete temperature and delivery status. Attach the ticket photo to the same delivery record rather than leaving it in the camera roll.

04

3. Tests and observations

Record the test type, result, unit, time, sample reference and notes required by your project. Supporting photos can help explain the field record, but the report does not replace certified test results.

05

4. Issues, actions and photos

Describe delays, rejected or adjusted deliveries, placement issues and other notable events while details are fresh. Record the action taken and attach relevant photos. Keep factual observations separate from conclusions that require formal review.

06

5. Closeout and handover

Before closing the pour, check that the expected delivery tickets, temperatures, discharge times, tests, issues and general photos have been recorded. Missing optional information should be shown clearly rather than silently filled in.
