PRACTICAL FIELD GUIDE

How to Document a Concrete Pour

Good pour documentation starts before the first truck and ends with a record another person can understand without having been on site.

Field-documentation guide. This page is a practical recordkeeping resource, not a project specification, engineering instruction, compliance certification or substitute for your approved QA/QC procedures.
01

Start with a clear pour identity

Create one record for the specific pour. Include the project, element or location, date, supplier, mix reference and ordered volume. A consistent naming convention makes completed pours easier to retrieve later.

02

Record each delivery as it arrives

Treat each truck as its own record. Capture its ticket or delivery number, volume and relevant timing information, then attach the ticket image directly to that delivery. This prevents the common end-of-day job of matching photos to handwritten notes.

03

Add temperatures, tests and sample references

Enter required field measurements and tests when they are taken. Keep the result, unit, time, notes and sample reference together. Use the requirements in your project documents to determine which tests are required and how they are evaluated.

04

Document exceptions while they are fresh

Delays, adjustments, rejected loads, placement interruptions and other issues are easier to describe accurately at the time. Record the event and the action taken, and use photos where they add useful context.

05

Check completeness before closing

Review the expected documentation before the crew moves on. Look for missing tickets, times, temperatures, tests or photos. A completeness check is about record coverage; it is not a statement that the concrete complies with the specification.

06

Turn the field record into the handover

The final report should be built from the same information captured during the pour. Keeping the source records connected reduces retyping and makes it easier for reviewers to follow individual deliveries and events.
